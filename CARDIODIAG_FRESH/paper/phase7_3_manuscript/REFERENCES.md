# References and literature verification

Citation metadata below was checked against primary or official records on 14 November 2026. The related-work discussion uses the cited sources only for claims their records support. This is a targeted literature check, not a systematic review, and the manuscript does not claim priority for applying machine learning to ZAS.

1. **Alizadehsani R, Habibi J, Hosseini MJ, et al.** A data mining approach for diagnosis of coronary artery disease. *Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine*. 2013;111(1):52–61. doi:[10.1016/j.cmpb.2013.03.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2013.03.004). Primary article record checked via [PubMed/National Library of Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23537611/). Supports the original ZAS dataset/classification context; it does not verify timing in the current local CSV.
2. **Alizadehsani R, Roshanzamir M, Sani Z.** Z-Alizadeh Sani [dataset]. UCI Machine Learning Repository. doi:[10.24432/C5Q31T](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5Q31T). Official [UCI dataset page](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/412/z). The page lists 303 instances, the >=50% diameter-narrowing outcome definition, and says 54 features; the manuscript flags local schema discrepancy.
3. **Cawley GC, Talbot NLC.** On over-fitting in model selection and subsequent selection bias in performance evaluation. *Journal of Machine Learning Research*. 2010;11:2079–2107. [Publisher paper](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html). Supports the general model-selection optimism rationale.
4. **Van Calster B, McLernon DJ, van Smeden M, Wynants L, Steyerberg EW.** Calibration: the Achilles heel of predictive analytics. *BMC Medicine*. 2019;17:230. doi:[10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7). Supports distinction between discrimination and calibration and the need for adequate calibration assessment.
5. **Collins GS, Moons KGM, Dhiman P, et al.** TRIPOD+AI statement: updated guidance for reporting clinical prediction models that use regression or machine learning methods. *BMJ*. 2024;385:e078378. doi:[10.1136/bmj-2023-078378](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078378). Supports reporting transparency; it is not a quality certification.
6. **Moons KGM, Damen JAA, Kaul T, et al.** PROBAST+AI: an updated quality, risk of bias, and applicability assessment tool for prediction models using regression or artificial intelligence methods. *BMJ*. 2025;388:e082505. doi:[10.1136/bmj-2024-082505](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-082505). Supports separate consideration of methodological quality, risk of bias and applicability.
7. **Janosi A, Steinbrunn W, Pfisterer M, Detrano R.** Heart Disease [dataset]. UCI Machine Learning Repository. doi:[10.24432/C52P4X](https://doi.org/10.24432/C52P4X). Official [UCI dataset page](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/45/heart+disease). Documents the four databases, missing values, outcome field and original attribute definitions.
8. **Davis J, Goadrich M.** The relationship between Precision-Recall and ROC curves. In: *Proceedings of the 23rd International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML)*. 2006:233–240. doi:[10.1145/1143844.1143874](https://doi.org/10.1145/1143844.1143874). Supports interpreting precision-recall results in relation to class distribution.
9. **Detrano R, Janosi A, Steinbrunn W, et al.** International application of a new probability algorithm for the diagnosis of coronary artery disease. *The American Journal of Cardiology*. 1989;64(5):304–310. doi:[10.1016/0002-9149(89)90524-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0002-9149(89)90524-9). The UCI repository identifies this as the introductory paper for the Heart Disease dataset.
