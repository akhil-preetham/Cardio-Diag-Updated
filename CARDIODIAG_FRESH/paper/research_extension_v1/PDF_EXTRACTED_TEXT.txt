Retrospective Coronary Artery Disease
Discrimination

Abstract—High cardiovascular classiﬁcation scores do not
establish that model selection is evaluated fairly, that probabil-
ities are calibrated, or that performance transports to another
clinical setting. We extend an existing CardioDiag-derived re-
search project through information-matched controls, selection-
independent calibration and a new public-cohort transport ex-
periment. The source Z-Alizadeh Sani (ZAS) resource contains
303 source rows with 216 CAD labels. Fifteen paired outer
partitions evaluate the historical selection procedure, a 38-feature
logistic control, information-group ablations and a corrected
three-component calibration ensemble. Historical selection and
equal-feature logistic mean AUCs were 0.9103 and 0.9174; cor-
rected raw/calibrated ensemble Brier scores were 0.1055/0.1037.
A separately frozen model using Age, Sex, diabetes, hypertension
and current smoking averaged source ROC-AUC 0.8059 and
achieved 0.7125 on 377 matched AngioCAD cases; its record-
bootstrap interval was 0.6474-0.7730. Target probability accuracy
deteriorated, and simulated source-feature deletion reduced mean
AUC from 0.8059 to 0.6875 at a 50% cell-mask rate. Endpoint,
timing, coding and patient-independence limitations preclude a
clinical-generalization claim. The contribution is a reproducible
empirical assessment of what drives discrimination and where
reliability fails, with preserved historical evidence and indepen-
dently checked outputs.
Index Terms—Coronary artery disease, nested validation, cali-
bration, external transport, information availability, reproducibil-
ity.
I. I NTRODUCTION
Cardiovascular classiﬁcation projects often begin with a
public table, a collection of classiﬁers and a search for a high
accuracy or area under the receiver-operating-characteristic
curve (ROC-AUC). A useful research question requires more:
the outcome must be deﬁned, the predictors must correspond
to a stated information set, selection must be evaluated as part
of the training procedure, and the interpretation must match
the population studied. A model can rank cases reasonably
while providing inaccurate probabilities or losing performance
in another setting.
The original CardioDiag paper by Khan, Raza and Nadeem
integrates percentile-based preprocessing, localized random
afﬁne shadow sampling, explanation-guided hybrid dimension-
ality reduction and optimized voting/stacking ensembles [1].
It already reports tuning, explainability, calibration, decision
curves, subgroup analyses and ablations. Adding another clas-
siﬁer or explanation ﬁgure is therefore a weak basis for a
subsequent novelty claim. This project instead examines the
evaluation and reliability of a CardioDiag-derived research
workﬂow, attributing the original architectures to their authors.
Earlier local work evaluated a feature-group/model-selection
procedure on ZAS against a ﬁve-feature V3-style reference.
Mean repeat AUC was approximately 0.910 versus 0.736.
However, the procedures differed in both available information
and model selection, while calibration inputs were selected
using labels from their later held-out calibration blocks. These
observations motivate two distinct controls: a simple model
with the same rich feature set, and a comparison in which
calibration is separated from the entire selection process.
The present extension also asks whether a compact shared-
feature model transports to AngioCAD, a separately released
clinical/angiographic resource [4]. The experiment deliberately
reports endpoint and spectrum discrepancies rather than treat-
ing all public heart-disease labels as equivalent. A further
stress test deletes held-out predictor values without reﬁtting the
model. These studies evaluate a reusable research procedure;
they do not demonstrate a universal cardiovascular diagnostic
system.
The contributions are: (i) a paired information-matched
logistic control and ﬁxed information-group ablations; (ii) a
fully rerun selection procedure within independent calibration-
training blocks, with an identical-component raw ensemble
control; (iii) source-only development followed by frozen,
compatibility-constrained AngioCAD evaluation and endpoint
sensitivities; and (iv) versioned predictions, input/output
hashes, split traces, tests and a reproducible compiled report.
These are applied methodological and empirical contributions,
not inventions of nested validation, sigmoid calibration or
logistic regression.

II. R ELATED WORK AND RESEARCH QUESTIONS
Cawley and Talbot describe overﬁtting of ﬁnite-sample se-
lection criteria and the resulting bias in performance evaluation
[2]. Evaluating the winning conﬁguration after comparing
many alternatives on the same data can underestimate selection
uncertainty. Nested validation evaluates choices made within
training, but it cannot erase earlier researcher adaptation after
exploring a dataset. Accordingly, all new ZAS analyses here
are explicitly retrospectively motivated follow-ups on previ-
ously studied source rows and outer assignments.
Calibration concerns the agreement between predicted prob-
abilities and observed event frequencies, while discrimination
concerns ordering [5]. A high AUC is therefore insufﬁcient
for reliable individual risk estimates. Model updating may
be needed when populations change, but updating with tar-
get labels must be distinguished from untouched transport
evaluation. We perform no AngioCAD label-based adaptation.
External-evaluation guidance also requires attention to the
target population, predictor deﬁnitions and outcome ascertain-
ment [8]. V alidation sample size should reﬂect the desired
precision of performance measures rather than a universal
case-count rule [9]. TRIPOD+AI and PROBAST+AI provide
reporting and appraisal frameworks [6], [7]; completing an
artifact or checklist does not imply low bias or clinical
applicability.
Four research questions structure the extension. RQ1 asks
whether rich-feature automated selection improves on a ﬁxed
38-feature regularized logistic procedure. RQ2 asks how the
calibration operation changes probability accuracy when ap-
plied to the same selected base components. RQ3 asks how
removing symptom/examination information, or restricting the
information set to risk plus symptoms, changes discrimination.
RQ4 asks how a new shared-core source model behaves under
cohort change and simulated missing inputs. No acceptance
criterion requires an AUC increase, and the full locked com-
parisons are retained regardless of direction.
III. D ATA, P ROVENANCE AND CLINICAL SCOPE
A. ZAS Source Data
The validated ZAS CSV contains 303 source rows and 56
columns, comprising 55 non-target ﬁelds and Cath. Cath is
mapped as Cad = 1 and Normal = 0, producing 216 positive
and 87 negative rows, with prevalence 0.7129. UCI describes
CAD using coronary diameter narrowing of at least 50%
[3]. The current provider workbook has the same 303-by-
56 schema. A fresh ofﬁcial archive download matches the
project’s raw archive byte for byte; its workbook is also byte-
identical, and the ﬁrst worksheet equals the validated CSV in
column order, inferred dtypes and values.
This resolves the question of an unexplained local extra
ﬁeld. The remaining discrepancy is between provider prose de-
scribing 54 features and the provider workbook containing 55
non-target ﬁelds. An empty second worksheet with 100 header
columns is a template, not additional clinical observations. No
full-record or all-predictor exact duplicate rows were found.
These checks do not establish unique-patient independence:
veriﬁed identiﬁers and visit linkage are unavailable. The
evaluation unit remains a source row.
B. AngioCAD Acquisition and Cohort Flow
Only the two small clinical-feature and label workbooks
from the ofﬁcial open CC BY 4.0 release were acquired; image
archives were unnecessary [4]. Provider byte sizes and MD5
checksums matched, and local SHA-256 hashes were retained.
The label table contains 413 unique case IDs, whereas the
clinical table contains 377. All 377 clinical IDs match labels;
the remaining 36 label cases lack clinical records. The release
does not explain that absence in the inspected metadata, so
selection associated with missing clinical records cannot be
excluded.
The operational positive target is any of 15 coronary seg-
ments labelled 51-75, 76-90, 91-99 or 100 percent steno-
sis. This yields 298 positive and 79 nonpositive matched
cases. The available 26-50 category cannot identify exact
50% narrowing, preventing reconstruction of the exact source
>=50% boundary. Ten of the 79 nonpositive cases contain
that ambiguous category. They are handled by prespeciﬁed
exclusion and upper-assignment sensitivity analyses; neither
assignment is asserted to recover their true source-equivalent
labels.
Matched clinical diagnoses comprise 186 STEMI, 20
NSTEMI, 55 unstable-angina and 116 stable-angina records.
Nonpositive stenosis cases are therefore not necessarily nor-
mal or healthy controls. They represent a different coronary-
referral spectrum from a population-screening sample. Case
IDs are release keys, not independent veriﬁcation that no
individual has multiple visits. Independent review used a
separate spreadsheet parser and conﬁrmed the joins, severity
vocabulary and agreement between vessel labels embedded in
the feature workbook and the separate label workbook.

C. Predictor Eligibility and Harmonization
The historical 38-ﬁeld group contains seven risk ﬁelds, ten
symptom/examination ﬁelds, seven resting-ECG ﬁelds and
fourteen laboratory ﬁelds. The original V3-style comparator
uses Age, Sex, Current Smoker, EX-Smoker and FH. EF-
TTE, Region RWMA and VHD remain excluded as potentially
downstream or diagnostic; Cath and direct angiographic out-
come ﬁelds are not predictors. None of the retained source
ﬁelds has source-conﬁrmed collection time before the diag-
nostic decision. The task is retrospective discrimination rather
than prospective early prediction.

AngioCAD lacks the complete V3 and rich symptom/ECG
ﬁelds. Its new allowlisted core is Age, Sex, DM, HTN and
Current Smoker, which replaces V3’s former-smoking and
family-history ﬁelds with diabetes and hypertension. It is
a new model, not a transported historical V3 or 38-ﬁeld
ﬁtted model. Age is used as numeric years; Male/Female are
explicitly mapped, including the literal ZAS spelling Fmale.
Binary 0/1 codes are retained directly without outcome-guided
inversion. Accessible target materials lack a complete ascer-
tainment/timing and binary-direction dictionary, so semantic
equivalence remains an assumption.
No laboratory units were inferred merely from similar col-
umn names. Diagnostic labels, vessel severity, Gensini score,
L VEF, procedure-series and medication ﬁelds are excluded
from the shared model. Age has six missing target entries,
and current smoking has 46; the corresponding source ﬁelds
have none missing. Source-training medians/modes are applied
unchanged at evaluation. Three target ages fall outside the
observed source age range of 30-86 years. No outcome-
dependent complete-case exclusion or target-based imputation
is performed.
IV. E XPERIMENTAL DESIGN
A. Frozen Source Evaluation
The extension retains the ﬁrst three repeats of the historical
ﬁve-fold outer assignments, giving 15 paired partitions and 303
out-of-fold predictions per procedure per repeat. Each outer-
training set has approximately 242 rows. Historical three-fold
inner partitions use seed 61051 + 100 times repeat + fold. All
transformations and hyperparameter selection are conﬁned to
their relevant training subsets. Outer labels are excluded from
training-function arguments; the new runners drop Cath from
input frames before ﬁtting.
The historical pool consists of six feature groups and ﬁve
unique model families: L2 logistic, elastic-net logistic, spline
logistic, random forest and histogram gradient boosting. There
are 30 group-family candidates and 120 parameter settings,
with deterministic tie order. The installed scikit-learn API and
package versions are recorded; an interior l1_ratio with saga
is interpreted as elastic net in that API. The original dtype-
based preprocessing is retained for comparability. Numeric-
coded categories can enter numeric/spline branches, an ac-
knowledged limitation rather than a silently revised algorithm.
B. Equal-Feature and Information Controls
The information-matched control uses ordinary L2 logistic
regression with all 38 ﬁelds and C in {0.1, 1, 10}. The exact
historical pipeline, inner partitions, post-selection cross-ﬁtted
sigmoid and Y ouden-J threshold construction are retained.
This preserves the same calibration limitations deliberately,
allowing a more interpretable comparison with saved historical
selection results. It is not the independently calibrated arm.
Three additional ﬁxed-family controls use 28 ﬁelds after
dropping all ten symptom/examination ﬁelds; 17 ﬁelds com-
prising risk plus symptoms/examination, excluding ECG and
laboratory information; and the ﬁve V3 ﬁelds with ordinary
unweighted L2. Full38 is reused rather than ﬁtted twice. The
ordinary ﬁve-ﬁeld ablation is distinguished from historical
class-balanced V3. These comparisons address information
content under common modeling rules, and do not identify
causal effects of individual clinical measurements.
C. Selection-Independent Calibration Ensemble
For each outer-training set T, the three inner blocks are
reused as calibration sets C_k, and D_k is the remaining
development subset. The entire 30-candidate search is rerun
using new three-fold validation only within D_k. Neither the
base estimator nor its group/family/parameter choice uses C_k
labels. The selected pipeline reﬁtted on D_k scores C_k, and a
ﬁxed weakly regularized sigmoid with C = 1,000,000 is ﬁtted
to raw base probabilities there.
The exact ﬁtted D_k base model is retained with its C_k
calibrator; it is never swapped for a model subsequently
reﬁtted on all of T. Each pair predicts the outer-test rows, and
the three calibrated probabilities are averaged. A paired raw
ensemble averages the probabilities from those identical base
components. Only this latter contrast isolates the calibration
operation. Comparing the new ensemble with the historical
single-reﬁt procedure also changes development sample size,
averaging and repeated selection, so it cannot be described as
a pure calibration-effect experiment.
Calibration blocks contain roughly 80 rows. Independence
of their labels from component selection does not guaran-
tee precise sigmoid estimation or good probability accuracy.
The ﬁxed C is large and supplies weak regularization; it is
not evidence of adequate small-sample regularization. New
corrected arms use a ﬁxed 0.5 threshold for descriptive sen-
sitivity/speciﬁcity, without a screening or clinical-cost inter-
pretation. No calibrator, threshold or family was added after
observing outer performance.
D. Source-Only Shared-Core Development and Transport
The ﬁve-core transport study has its own frozen protocol
and preprocessing dictionary. Unbalanced L2 logistic C in
{0.1, 1, 10} is selected by three-fold source-training AUC.
The same 15 outer source partitions estimate its within-ZAS
performance. A second source arm wraps the entire search
inside each training block of a three-fold sigmoid calibration
ensemble. Each ﬁtted search and its held-block calibrator
remain paired. The two transport arms differ in calibration,
ensemble averaging and training size, so their contrast is not
solely attributable to calibration.
After source evaluation, both procedures are ﬁtted using
source rows only. Model ﬁles and target probabilities are
hashed before target outcomes are scored. Target labels do not
select predictors, C, preprocessing, calibration or thresholds,
and are not used to update either model. The principal target
deﬁnition is the recorded >50% rule. Prespeciﬁed sensitivities
exclude ten boundary-ambiguous cases or assign those ten
positive as an upper assignment. All scenarios use the same
frozen probabilities.

E. Missingness Stress and Statistical Summaries
The raw shared-core source pipeline is also evaluated af-
ter independent per-cell deletion at rates 0, 0.10, 0.25 and
0.50 in held-out inputs. Masks have deterministic seeds, are
independent of outcomes and are saved. The ﬁtted pipeline is
not reﬁtted, and training-derived imputers handle the removed
values. One ﬁxed mask realization per rate/fold is used; this
is a simulated MCAR stress test, not evidence of robustness
to actual clinical missing-not-at-random processes.
ROC-AUC, average precision (AP), Brier score, log loss and
calibration diagnostics are reported. AP is interpreted relative
to event prevalence. Calibration intercept/slope are jointly
estimated logistic-regression coefﬁcients on logit probabilities,
not an intercept with unit slope ﬁxed. Source metrics are
calculated from complete-repeat out-of-fold predictions rather
than averages of fold AUCs. All three repeat results and their
descriptive means/ranges are retained; overlapping repeats do
not provide independent population replicates.
For the ﬁxed transported models, 2,000 record-resampling
percentile bootstrap draws summarize target metric uncertainty.
They condition on the source model and available target
records, excluding source-reﬁtting, patient-cluster and hospital-
sampling uncertainty. Bootstrap convergence and usable draw
counts are saved. No population-level signiﬁcance test or
independent-patient inference is based on repeated folds. A
separately labelled post hoc diagnostic compares target proba-
bility loss with a constant equal to source prevalence; it does
not select or update any ﬁtted model.

The equal-feature result gives a selection-minus-simple-
model AUC difference of -0.0071; the simple rich-feature
logistic control had higher AUC in all three observed repeats.
Thus these results do not establish an automated-selection
advantage. They change the interpretation of the much larger
historical gain over the sparse ﬁve-ﬁeld reference: additional
clinical information is a substantial competing explanation.
Removing the ten symptom/examination ﬁelds lowered mean
AUC by 0.0709, while retaining only 17 risk-plus-symptom
ﬁelds yielded AUC 0.9036 versus 0.9174 for all 38. The
latter compact procedure had Brier 0.1025, close descriptively
to the 38-ﬁeld control’s 0.1011, without an equivalence or
noninferiority claim. These restrictions concern recorded in-
formation, not measurement timing or causal necessity. The
corrected calibrated-versus-raw Brier difference of -0.0017
similarly separates implementation independence from em-
pirical probability accuracy; a proper separation design is
necessary evidence hygiene, not a guarantee of improvement.
The new cross-cohort experiment is stronger evidence than
treating another random seed as external validation. It uses
a genuinely separate public resource and source-only ﬁts,
while retaining compatibility constraints explicitly. However,
it validates neither the historical V3 weights nor the rich-
feature ZAS model. Its particular value is empirical evidence
of degradation: AUC falls, calibration changes and probability
loss is poor relative to a simple post hoc source-prevalence
diagnostic. A reliable scientiﬁc report includes this failure
boundary rather than modifying models until the target score
improves.
B. Relationship to the Base Project
The supplied base paper explicitly states training-fold-only
preprocessing and a 75:25 ﬁnal holdout. Static review of
a traceable released notebook identiﬁes speciﬁc paths with
full-data LoRAS and PCA before a later split, and target-
conditioned imputation. Local archive Git-blob hashes match
the public release and the inspected notebook matches its
archive member. These are concrete source/report discrepan-
cies, but the notebook’s execution state and provenance of
every published table were not reconstructed; they do not
justify declaring every original published result invalid.
The present analysis does not claim to beat the original
paper’s approximately 0.997 AUC on its different Five States
or Mendeley tasks. Nor does it claim to have invented the
original OVEC, OSEC or XAIH-DRC components. Corrected
6
architecture-speciﬁc head-to-head experiments on identical
admissible records would be needed for an original-ensemble
superiority claim. The contribution reported here is selection-
aware information and reliability evaluation with new clinical-
cohort evidence.
The historical four-centre UCI study is also retained as a
separate task. Reconstructing a heart-disease cohort or using
a new ﬁle name does not make it an independent ZAS
validator. Existing Heart5/Statlog/UCI amalgams can overlap
in provenance. By keeping these tracks separate and acquiring
provider-released AngioCAD tables, the extension avoids a
misleading combined leaderboard across incompatible labels
and reused records.
C. Research Interface and Reproducibility
A small research scoring interface accompanies the frozen
ﬁve-core source model. It requires exactly the ﬁve declared
ﬁelds, checks adult age bounds and categorical/binary codes,
rejects empty or entirely missing records, and preserves
missing values for the source-trained imputers. It reports
missing-ﬁeld counts and age-range ﬂags alongside probabili-
ties, without a diagnosis label. The trusted local model bundle
is checked against its prediction-freeze hash before loading.
These controls prevent schema mistakes; they do not certify a
patient-speciﬁc prediction or detect every form of distribution
shift.
The full candidate experiment uses one BLAS/OpenMP
thread and one search job to control local resources. The
ﬁrst outer partition took 85.07 seconds, projecting roughly

VII. L IMITATIONS AND RESPONSIBLE INTERPRETATION
First, ZAS is small relative to searching many groups
and families. The follow-up reuses previously explored rows
and outer assignments, so it is not prospectively independent
conﬁrmation. Second, source-row independence is unveriﬁed
in both resources. Unique release IDs and zero exact duplicates
cannot establish independent patients or support patient-cluster
uncertainty. Third, predictor acquisition timing is unresolved;
retrospective symptoms and measurements may encode diag-
nostic work-up or referral rather than information available at
an earlier clinical decision.
Fourth, the historical information controls retain selection-
dependent calibration and training-size mismatch for compara-
bility, while the corrected sigmoid maps use small calibration
samples and weak regularization. Fifth, the target endpoint
is >50% by coarse categories, whereas source documentation
states >=50%. Sensitivity assignments expose but do not ﬁx
that discrepancy. Sixth, absence of 36 target clinical records,
acute/stable case mix, missing smoking/age and incompletely
documented binary ascertainment constrain cross-cohort inter-
pretation. No target recalibration was undertaken to conceal
these limitations.
Seventh, three overlapping source repeats and ﬁxed-model
target record bootstraps cover limited uncertainty components.
They do not estimate arbitrary-hospital transport or full de-
velopment uncertainty. Eighth, simulated MCAR deletion is
not a model of every clinical missingness mechanism. Ninth,
thresholds have no clinician-deﬁned decision cost, and no
clinical-utility, fairness certiﬁcation or prospective beneﬁt is
established. Finally, no novel-classiﬁer priority or superiority
to original ensembles is claimed without the relevant matched
experiments.
The appropriate intended use is methodological research
and retrospective cohort analysis. A deployed clinical system
would require a well-deﬁned decision point, source-conﬁrmed
predictor timing, independent patient-level evaluation, compat-
ible outcome adjudication, local calibration and a separately
justiﬁed clinical-use study. The completed computational work
provides evidence and failure boundaries, rather than a guar-
antee of reliable answers for arbitrary datasets.
VIII. C ONCLUSION AND PROJECT DISPOSITION
The computational extension completed information-
matched controls, 45 selection-independent calibration
components, three information-group ablations and a
separate frozen shared-core transport study. The observed
selection-minus-equal-feature AUC difference was -0.0071;
the corrected calibration-operation Brier difference was
-0.0017. These results reﬁne the historical high-score claim by
7
identifying the roles of available information and probability
construction.
The new public-cohort experiment demonstrates the limits
of source-only transport, and the missingness stress test shows
that generating an output after imputation does not preserve
predictive information. This makes information-matched con-
trols, probability loss and explicit data compatibility central to
the contribution. The computational extension, audit artifacts
and compiled report are complete within the stated retrospec-
tive scope. Clinical deployment, prospective validation and
universal reliability remain unestablished.
